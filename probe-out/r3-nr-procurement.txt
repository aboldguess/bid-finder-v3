<h2 class="wp-block-heading has-xx-large-font-size">Procurement opportunities and processes</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We procure approximately £7bn worth of goods, works and services each year. Some of our procurements are managed by commercial and procurement colleagues in our Route Services function. These tend to be for things which are best provided for the whole company from a single team. For example, materials such as ballast and IT services.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Commercial and Procurement colleagues in our regions manage procurement which is specific to their region, for example, capital works.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We procure from a range of suppliers <a href="https://www.networkrail.co.uk/industry-and-commercial/supply-chain/information-for-smes/">including SMEs</a> and <a href="https://www.networkrail.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Key-delivery-partners-map-June-25.pdf">key delivery partners</a>.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Procurement Act (2023)</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Procurement Act (2023) came into force on 24 February 2025. It brings the biggest changes to public procurements in a generation and replaces the Utilities Contracts Regulations.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We are bound by the Act, and so are suppliers who participate in our new procurements. Suppliers should refer to our <a href="https://www.networkrail.co.uk/industry-and-commercial/supply-chain/procurement-act-2023/">Procurement Act 2023 page</a> for the latest information.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Categories of goods, services and works</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most of our spending is split across different categories of goods, services and works. We manage these categories to reflect the supply market and our requirements, to ensure best value for money and to deliver a sustainable and resilient rail service.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Network Rail standards define which categories of goods, services or works need suppliers to be audited, and which are non-auditable. Our category scopes and codes match with RISQS codes, called <a href="https://www.risqs.org/riccl">RICCL codes</a> (Railway Industry Commodity Classification List). This is so our direct suppliers can more easily understand when they need to register with RISQS to supply Network Rail in our auditable categories.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We’re currently refreshing how we share these codes and will give an update here shortly. For now, please look on <a href="https://www.risqs.org/suppliers/suppliers-faqs">RISQS Supplier FAQs</a> to find out about the codes and auditable status.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Procurement pipeline</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We’re committed to making our procurement pipeline visible. We recognise that sharing our future procurement requirements with our suppliers helps them to plan their business and resource appropriately to support our requirements.</p>
